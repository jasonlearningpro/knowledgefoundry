Add a shadow to text, pictures and shapes

A shadow makes an item stand out from what is behind it. It only decorates: it is drawn outside the item, so nothing moves or changes size, and screen readers ignore it.

  1. Select a text box, a picture or a shape. Buttons do not have shadows.
  2. Click Shadow in the tools beside the item (the floating palette, or the row of tools if you docked it), or find Shadow in Properties. Both show the same settings. Tick Show a shadow.
  3. Change what you like: • Color: pick a theme color or any other color. Black is the default. • Horizontal and Vertical: how far the shadow falls sideways and downward, in pixels. A positive number moves it right or down; a negative number moves it left or up. • Blur: how soft the edge is. 0 is a hard edge. • Opacity: how strong it is, from 0% to 100%.

The same settings in Properties:

The shadow follows the real shape. A shadow on text goes behind the letters, and a shadow on a picture with see-through parts follows the picture, not its box. It is also copied when you copy, paste or duplicate the item, and it stays on small screens.

Good to know. Every item starts with the shadow off. A shadow is cut off at the edge of the screen, so leave some room if an item is close to it. Keep it soft and light behind text: a strong, dark shadow can make words harder to read.
